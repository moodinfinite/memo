## November 19 release — daily alley practice and study updates

The Lane offers a ten-minute daily practice on the 1,093 TOCFL Level 3 words. It mixes due reviews (including Familiar words) with up to three introductions. The planner allows eight minutes for cards, budgeting one minute per review and two per new word, with room to use a word in a phrase. These are initial estimates. Evaluate them against later recall and actual session duration before drawing conclusions about their effectiveness. Five sections beneath the scene show familiar-word progress. The 35 lights in the illustration still switch on at their original thresholds.

The regular Daily 10 draws from personal sets. Master Deck now includes Learn, set study shows resumable progress, flashcard editing is centered, and keyboard answers use the button feedback. The sidebar resource library includes public resources and reads the tutor sheet from the signed-in account’s bookmark.

- Last deployed commit: see `git log` (12 November) — **level check rebuilt on Taiwan's TOCFL scale** after the old HSK test placed Jeffrey at HSK 5 while he can barely read a menu (research: `docs/level-check-research.md`). Levels are Novice, 1–6 (`TOCFL_LEVELS` in `src/lib/levelTest.ts`, honest CEFR-based can-dos). Part 1: typed English meanings for 20–30 words sampled by level from the official TOCFL list (`public/tocfl.txt`, built by `scripts/build-tocfl.mjs`); about 70% of picks avoid words in the user's flashcards; the app matches answers against CC-CEDICT senses and Claude grades the rest in batches (`meanings` action). Scoring is one Bayesian ability estimate over all answers (slope 1.7, guess floor 0 for typed, 1/4 for multiple choice, prior centred low), starting at Level 1 every time; it stops after 20 words once the estimate is tight (max 30) and reports the level reached with 70% probability, with a range. Part 2: two passages Claude writes around list words (`passage` action) at the estimated level, then one up or down; the app rejects passages with more than 12% above-level words; `verify` drops questions whose key a second pass disputes or that it can answer from the options alone; a loose timer gives half credit after time. Part 3: a real-world text (menu, notice, news) rated Almost all / About half / Very little; Very little lowers the result one level. Writing is graded separately on the same scale and doesn't move the level. Old HSK results stay in history as "old test" but don't count for the badge, stats or due date (so the new check shows as due). All thresholds are untested assumptions; the simulation in `tests/level-test.test.mjs` checks it never overrates a modelled learner by two levels. The live endpoint has not been run end to end yet; it was tested in the preview with a mocked API.
